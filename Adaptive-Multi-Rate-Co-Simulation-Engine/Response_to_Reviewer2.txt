COMMENT 2: Delineation of Original Contributions Against Prior Art
"The authors should clearly state which parts of the adaptive multi-rate 
cosimulation approach is really their original contribution, notably 
against references 19 [Gomes et al.] and 20 [Schweiger et al.]."

RESPONSE:
We thank the reviewer for highlighting the need for clearer attribution. We 
have added a dedicated subsection explicitly delineating our original 
contributions across four distinct axes against Gomes et al. [19] and 
Schweiger et al. [20].

CHANGES MADE IN MANUSCRIPT:
- Added Section 1.3 ("Scope, Original Contributions, and Relation to Prior Work", 
  lines 131–138), delineating:
  1. Continuous-Discrete Cross-Domain Coupling: Gomes et al. and Schweiger 
     et al. address continuous-continuous DAE-to-DAE coupling (e.g., automotive 
     and power systems). Our work solves the fundamentally distinct problem 
     of coupling continuous astrodynamics ODE integrators to discrete-event 
     (DEVS) network packet queues across CCSDS ModCod switching thresholds.
  2. Predictive Look-Ahead vs. Reactive Rollback: Standard co-simulation master 
     algorithms surveyed in [20] handle event zero-crossings via reactive ODE 
     state rollbacks. In contrast, our engine proactively contracts the coupling 
     interval ahead of line-of-sight occultations and SNR gradients, eliminating 
     event timing delay without rollback computation.
  3. Domain-Specific Multi-Rate Regimes with Event Guards: We formulate an 
     astrodynamics-aware step hierarchy (DT_MIN = 1.0 s, DT_COAST = 600 s, 
     DT_BLACKOUT = 3600 s) combined with a predictive boundary event guard 
     that prevents boundary overshoot and step chatter.
  4. Relativistic Spacetime Limits: We formally derive the theoretical 
     simultaneity and proper-time drift bounds (Fidelity Gaps 2 & 3) that are 
     absent from existing co-simulation literature.

COMMENT 3: Correction of Inaccurate Claims Regarding ns-3 in Table 1
"Table 1 claims that ns-3 'assumes zero/constant propagation delay'. This is 
factually incorrect, as ns-3 includes explicit propagation delay models such 
as ConstantSpeedPropagationDelayModel. Overstating limitations of standard tools 
damages the credibility of the review."

RESPONSE:
We thank the reviewer for pointing out this inaccurate statement. We have 
revised Table 1 and Section 2 to accurately articulate the true limitations 
of ns-3 and similar discrete-event network simulators when applied to deep space:
- ns-3 accurately models speed-of-light propagation delays between static or 
  kinematically simplified node pairs.
- However, ns-3 lacks relativistic frame transformations (TCB/TCG/TDB proper-time 
  mappings) and does not natively integrate dynamical orbital ephemerides 
  (SPICE kernels) with look-ahead synchronization.
- Consequently, network simulators require external astrodynamic ephemeris 
  traces, creating the cross-domain synchronization dilemma investigated in 
  this paper.

CHANGES MADE IN MANUSCRIPT:
- Table 1 (line 144): Replaced "Assumes zero/constant propagation delay" with:
  "Lacks relativistic frame transformations and dynamic orbital ephemerides 
   (SPICE look-ahead synchronization)."
- Section 2.2 (lines 175–190): Clarified the exact boundary between ns-3's 
  discrete networking capabilities and the continuous astrodynamics solver.

COMMENT 4: Figure Formatting and Canvas Cleanliness
"Figure captions should be under the figures... Some figures appear to have 
duplicate titles and overlapping axis text."

RESPONSE:
We agree with the reviewer. The visual overlap occurred because title text 
generated via Python's matplotlib `fig.suptitle()` was rendered directly on 
the PDF image canvas, appearing above the graphic while the LaTeX caption 
appeared below.

CHANGES MADE IN MANUSCRIPT:
- Removed all embedded `fig.suptitle()` titles from `simulation/generate_figures.py`.
- Regenerated all six vector figure PDFs (Figures 1–6) with clean canvas margins 
  and harmonious color palettes.
- Formatted `manuscript.tex` so that all captions are typeset exclusively below 
  each figure float using Elsevier's native `cas-sc.cls` float specifications.
- Verified that all figures render cleanly without any text overlap or label 
  clipping.
