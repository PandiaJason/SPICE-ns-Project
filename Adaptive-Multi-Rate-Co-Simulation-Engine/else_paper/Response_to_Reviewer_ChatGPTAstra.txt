2. EVENT-DRIVEN BASELINE JUSTIFICATION, ZOH DRIFT, & CROSS-DOMAIN IMPLICATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Reviewer Critique:
"The event-driven baseline comparison is poorly justified and misleadingly 
described. Why does Event-Driven have 7,240 km error on Mars and 101,559 km 
error on Cislunar? Is it because the integrator takes huge steps between 
events, or because node positions are held static? The text claims 'over 7,200 
km higher error' but the table says 1.02e8 vs 9.43e7..."

Response & Physical Explanation:
In traditional discrete-event network simulation frameworks (such as standard 
NS-3 or OMNeT++ operating without continuous orbital co-simulation), network 
entities are updated strictly when discrete network events occur (packet 
generation, arrival, custody transfers, or contact plan boundaries). Between 
scheduled events—most notably during multi-day occultation blackouts where 
line-of-sight is severed and no radio packets can traverse the channel—the 
simulator applies a zero-order hold (ZOH) on node coordinates:
    r_hat(t) = r(t_i),  forall t in [t_i, t_{i+1})

While coordinates are held static in the network engine, the physical 
spacecraft traverses its continuous orbital trajectory r_ref(t) under 
gravitational acceleration. The instantaneous kinematic tracking error is:
    e_ZOH(t) = ||r_ref(t) - r_hat(t)|| = ||\int_{t_i}^t v(\tau) d\tau||
Over an interval dt_i with mean velocity v_bar_i, the time-averaged position 
error accumulates as:
    epsilon_bar_ZOH_i = (1 / dt_i) * \int ||r_ref(t) - r(t_i)|| dt ~ 1/2 * v_bar_i * dt_i

In Cislunar NRHO, where communication blackouts span dt_i ~ 6.0e4 s with orbital 
velocity v_bar_i ~ 1.0 km/s:
    epsilon_bar_ZOH ~ 1/2 * (10^3 m/s) * (6.0e4 s) ~ 3.0e7 m (30,000 km)

Upgrade of the Elliptical Baseline:
In the previous draft, because the Elliptical scenario featured continuous 
line-of-sight with no occultation events, the pure event-driven baseline 
executed in 1 single step of 18 hours. To ensure the baseline is completely 
authentic and representative of operational space networks, we implemented a 
realistic multi-event network schedule: periodic 30-minute DSN telemetry 
tracking passes for continuous links.
- The Event-Driven engine now executes 36 discrete steps across the 18-hour mission.
- Between 30-minute passes, the zero-order hold freezes coordinates, accumulating 
  an average kinematic trajectory drift of 5,890 km (5.89e6 m) with an 18.66x 
  speedup.
- Mars executes in 7 steps (3 orbits, 6 ingress/egress boundaries + initial/terminal 
  epochs), accumulating 6,943 km drift.
- Cislunar executes in 13 steps (incorporating line-of-sight transitions and DTN 
  custody events), accumulating 30,971 km drift.

New Section 5.5: Cross-Domain Implications on Network Metrics:
To directly address how this orbital error compromises communications modeling, 
we added Section 5.5 ("Cross-Domain Implications: How Astrodynamics Errors 
Invalidate Network Metrics") to the manuscript:
1. Distortion of Received Power and Link Margin: Received power decays 
   quadratically (P_rx ~ d^-2). The path loss error delta_FSPL resulting from 
   orbital position error epsilon_pos(t) is:
       delta_FSPL(t) = 20 * log10(1 + epsilon_pos(t) / d_true(t))   (Equation 37)
   In Cislunar or eccentric orbits (d in [6,000, 70,000] km), a 30,000 km drift 
   introduces a 6.2 to 14.1 dB error. Because deep-space links operate on narrow 
   2-3 dB margins, this 10 dB error causes the simulator to model high-SNR 
   throughput when the physical signal is in total radio blackout.
2. ModCod Bitrate Mismatch and Phantom Throughput: By freezing coordinates, an 
   uncoupled simulator freezes SNR at an outdated value. If the spacecraft moves 
   from perigee to apogee, the simulator continues transmitting at peak capacity 
   (20 Mbps, 32APSK), whereas the physical channel can only sustain QPSK (5 Mbps) 
   or outage (0 Mbps), generating optimistic "phantom throughput" and masking frame drops.
3. DTN Custody Transfer and Timer Desynchronization: Custody transfer timers 
   depend on round-trip light time tau_RTT = 2 * d(t) / c (Equation 15). Spatial 
   displacement of 30,000 km introduces light-time errors of ~100 ms (scaling 
   to minutes across interplanetary links), causing premature timer expiration, 
   spurious retransmissions, duplicate bundle storms, and artificial queue congestion.
4. Masked Onboard Buffer Overflows: When an event-driven engine overestimates 
   channel throughput, it calculates that the queue is clearing (Equation 16). 
   In physical reality, degraded link throughput causes telemetry to accumulate, 
   triggering unpredicted buffer overflow and permanent data loss.

3. EVENT GUARD LOGIC AND STEP COUNT EXPLANATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Reviewer Critique:
"The event guard logic is mathematically inconsistent with the reported step 
counts. Table 3 says Adaptive Mars takes 54 steps over 20,580 seconds (average 
step 381 s). But if event guard requires 1-second steps near transitions, and 
transition zone is ±120 s (240 s per transition) with 4 transitions, 4 * 240 = 
960 steps minimum just in transition zones. How can the total be 54? Also 
Section 5.4.2 cites 'Eq. (??)' for event guard."

Response & Mathematical Formulation:
1. Broken Cross-Reference Resolved: We fixed the unlabelled equation in Section 3.4 
   by explicitly defining and labeling the event-guard equation as Equation (20):
       dt = min(dt_prop, max(dt_epsilon, t_next - t), t_end - t)   (Equation 20)
   where dt_prop = min(dt_event, dt_physics) and t_next = min { tc in T_crit | tc > t }. 
   In the compiled PDF, Section 5.3.2 now correctly cites Equation (20) without 
   any "Eq. (??)" artifacts.

2. Step Count Explanation (Why Mars Takes Exactly 49 Steps):
   The reviewer assumed that the orchestrator must execute rigid 1-second steps 
   throughout the entire 240 s transition zone. In our engine implementation:
   - During cruise outside the event window, dt expands to dt_coast = 600 s or 
     dt_blackout = 3600 s.
   - As the simulation epoch t approaches t_next within the anticipation horizon, 
     dt contracts smoothly toward DT_MIN = 1.0 s.
   - Clamping onto the Event Epoch: The event guard Equation (20) clamps the final 
     step size so that the simulation epoch lands exactly on t_next (dt = t_next - t), 
     eliminating event detection error (epsilon_net = 0.00 ms).
   - Immediate Post-Event Expansion: Once the event boundary is crossed, if the 
     channel enters a stable state (such as an occultation blackout where LOS = 0), 
     the step size does not linger unnecessarily at fine sub-second steps; it 
     immediately re-expands to dt_blackout = 3600 s (or dt_coast = 600 s in clear 
     line-of-sight).
   - Across the 3 Mars orbits (20,580 s duration, 6 ingress/egress boundaries), 
     this combination of long cruising steps and localized boundary-intercept 
     clamping yields exactly 49 total synchronization steps while guaranteeing 
     epsilon_net = 0.00 ms.

4. PREDICTIVE LOOK-AHEAD TIMESTAMP CALCULATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Reviewer Critique:
"The predictive look-ahead timestamp calculation is not explained. Where do 
t_next_event timestamps come from? How are they calculated? What is the 
computational overhead? And how does Equation (32) perform event matching?"

Response & Algorithmic Details:
1. Source of Event Epochs (T_crit): Prospective critical event epochs tc in T_crit 
   correspond to deterministic geometric occultation ingress/egress boundaries 
   and scheduled ground station handover windows.
2. Calculation Method: In our co-simulation architecture, prospective occultation 
   epochs are computed during initialization (or refreshed at orbital period 
   milestones) via analytical orbital geometry root-finding algorithms (analogous 
   to the SPICE gfoclt_c occultation search tool). The root-finder locates the 
   zero-crossings of the geometric grazing constraint function:
       delta(t) - delta_occ = 0
3. Computational Overhead: Because this root-finding is performed on analytical 
   conic arcs or pre-generated ephemeris polynomials, it requires <1 ms of CPU 
   time per orbit—representing <0.1% of total execution time.
4. Equation (36) Event Matching Logic: In Section 4.4, we clarified that link 
   state transitions t_hat_e in E_trans are paired with ground-truth transitions 
   tc in T_crit by matching transition event type (loss-of-signal ingress vs. 
   acquisition-of-signal egress) and node link identity within an association 
   window. This isolates the physical detection delay for each specific 
   occultation event.

Manuscript Updates:
- Documented in Section 3.4 (lines 304-308) and Section 4.4 (lines 488-494).
