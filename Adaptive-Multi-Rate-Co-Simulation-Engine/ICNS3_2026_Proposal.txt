The Abstract (Word count: 196 words -- strictly < 200 words)
================================================================================
Conventional Discrete-Event Simulation (DES) frameworks, such as ns-3, assume that
network states evolve exclusively via instantaneous, causal state transitions
triggered by discrete event queue pops. While highly effective for terrestrial and
near-Earth topologies, this paradigm collapses in deep space, where network
topology is a continuous function of time G(t) governed by n-body gravitational
motion, multi-minute light-time delays (up to 24 min on Mars), and planetary
occultations.

Standard DES faces an unavoidable dilemma: pure event-driven scheduling freezes
orbital dynamics during communication blackouts, accumulating over 30,970 km in
unphysical trajectory drift that severely distorts received power (by 6.2 to 14.1 dB)
and induces ModCod rate switching errors; conversely, fixed-step synchronization
introduces tens of seconds in boundary quantization delay (epsilon_net up to 32.7 s)
and massive computational waste. Furthermore, multi-minute propagation delays break
static delay modeling, requiring iterative light-cone solving for causal arrival
scheduling.

This lightning talk presents a critical architectural evaluation of DES fidelity in
interplanetary networking. We report empirical Pareto frontiers demonstrating
5.43x to 9.90x speedups with zero boundary error (epsilon_net = 0.00 ms) across three
canonical space profiles, verify sublinear scalability to 500 nodes, and propose
three concrete blueprints to extend ns-3 for solar-system-scale simulation.
