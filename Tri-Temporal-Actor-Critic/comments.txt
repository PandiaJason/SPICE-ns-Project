1. Please state in no more than 150 words why your work is important to other researchers in Artificial Intelligence and how they could make use of your results.

[Word Count: 141 words]
Reinforcement learning (RL) in physical cyber-physical systems—from robotics and edge computing to long-range autonomous exploration—faces severe operational failure due to state-dependent, non-uniform, and multi-minute communication latencies. Existing delay-compensating methods either explode computationally via state augmentation O(tau) or diverge under dynamic delay jitter.

Our work is important because it introduces the first continuous-time actor-critic framework (TTAC) that decouples forward state prediction (Neural ODEs) from backward credit assignment (temporal attention) without history concatenation, preserving a strictly constant O(1) input dimension and provably bounded gradient variance.

AI researchers can directly utilize:
1. The mathematical SD-NU-UDMDP formulation and multi-scale Bellman contraction proofs to study stability in partially observed and delayed environments;
2. The attention-based retrospective alignment mechanism to solve delayed reward attribution without handcrafted heuristics; and
3. Our modular, open-source codebase for zero-shot deployment in autonomous vehicles, teleoperation, and networked control experiencing extreme transmission delays.

3. Dietterich (2000), Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning with the MAXQ Value Function Decomposition (Vol. 13).

How our work differs:
Rajan et al. benchmarked agent vulnerability under delay but studied only discrete, stationary latencies without algorithmic remedies for dynamic, out-of-order jitter. Kaelbling et al. framed delayed feedback and credit assignment as open challenges, primarily tackled via Markovian state augmentation. Dietterich decomposed value functions across discrete subtasks without temporal delays.

TTAC fundamentally differs by: (i) resolving continuous-time, state-dependent, and out-of-order latency using Neural ODE trajectory integration and retrospective attention; (ii) eliminating state augmentation to maintain strictly constant O(1) complexity across thousands of delay steps; and (iii) proving multi-scale Bellman contraction and finite policy gradient variance bounds.
