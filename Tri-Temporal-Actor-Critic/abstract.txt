Tri-Temporal Actor-Critic: Continuous-Time Reinforcement Learning under Extreme and Non-Uniform Latency

Authors: Prof. Jason Pandian, Dr. I. Kala

Abstract:
In autonomous cyber-physical systems operating over planetary spatial scales or congested communication links—where feedback is constrained by huge light-time propagation delays scaling from seconds to tens of minutes—conventional reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms suffer a fundamental operational and mathematical collapse. Under such extreme latency horizons, reactive feedback is physically impossible, discrete state augmentation incurs catastrophic parameter explosion O(tau) across deep temporal horizons (scaling to thousands of unobserved decision steps), and discrete-time recurrent architectures fail to capture continuous physical kinematics over irregular arrival timestamps. In this paper, we formalize this challenge as the Continuous-Time State-Dependent, Non-Uniform, and Unbounded Delayed Markov Decision Process (SD-NU-UDMDP), and propose the Tri-Temporal Actor-Critic (TTAC) framework to overcome the curse of extreme latency without history augmentation. The foundational novelty of TTAC lies in causally decoupling the continuous physical state-estimation problem (Past -> Present) from the delayed credit-assignment problem (Future -> Past) through three synchronized temporal layers: (i) a continuous-time Predictive Layer using Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) to integrate latent system dynamics forward over variable, irregular delay intervals [t - tau_t, t], preserving a strictly constant O(1) policy input dimension; (ii) an instantaneous Present Layer executing real-time closed-loop control guided by an aligned generative pseudo-reward estimator; and (iii) a Retrospective Layer employing non-local temporal attention to causally redistribute delayed empirical returns back to past historical actions across out-of-order arrival sequences. Theoretically, we prove that the TTAC multi-scale Bellman operator is a strict gamma-contraction in the infinity norm on policy evaluation, and establish a Variance Bounding Theorem proving that stochastic policy gradient variance remains bounded by a finite constant under an exponential delay moment condition of order 2L_f. Evaluated across a three-tier physical operational hierarchy—continuous orbital trajectory tracking under distance-dependent relativistic propagation delay, asynchronous packet queue scheduling under out-of-order delivery, and continuous locomotion under velocity-dependent actuation delay—TTAC maintains a strictly constant O(1) policy input dimension, achieves sub-logarithmic state reconstruction error, bounds empirical gradient variance by over two orders of magnitude compared to uncompensated baselines, and outperforms constant-delay and state-augmented baselines by up to 15-fold to 240-fold in sample efficiency and tracking stability.

Keywords: Delayed Reinforcement Learning, Continuous-Time Control, Neural Ordinary Differential Equations, Extreme Latency, Non-Uniform Feedback, Tri-Temporal Actor-Critic, Deep-Space Communication, Cyber-Physical Systems.
